Technical Dossier

What Gets Logged Every Time You Drive Past a Flock Safety Camera

Each Flock Safety Falcon camera captures and transmits the following data to cloud storage accessible by subscribing law enforcement agencies, with no warrant, probable cause, or judicial oversight required for queries.

Vehicle Capture Data Points

License Plate OCR

Optical character recognition extracts plate number, state of issuance, and plate type classification.

Vehicle Identification

Make, model, generation, body type, and color, matched against a vehicle fingerprint database.

Distinguishing Features

Bumper stickers, roof racks, trailer hitches, dents, aftermarket modifications, logged as "Vehicle Fingerprint" attributes for re-identification even without a readable plate.

Timestamp & Travel Direction

GPS-synced timestamp, travel direction (N/S/E/W), and lane position for movement pattern analysis.

Contextual Image Capture

Full-frame photograph of the vehicle, occupant silhouettes, and surrounding traffic, retained alongside metadata.

Wireless Network Sniffing

Some Flock units detect Bluetooth and WiFi probe requests from in-vehicle and passenger devices, creating an additional device-level tracking vector.

Constitutional & Oversight Concerns

Fourth Amendment Search Protections

The Supreme Court ruled in Carpenter v. United States (2018) that prolonged tracking of an individual's movements constitutes a Fourth Amendment search. ALPR networks that aggregate location data over time may meet this threshold, yet no Arkansas municipality has enacted warrant requirements for ALPR queries. Officers query plates without individualized suspicion on a routine basis.

Cross-Jurisdiction & Out-of-State Access

Flock Safety's platform enables nationwide agency-to-agency data sharing. A plate scanned in Rogers, Arkansas can be queried by a department in Virginia, Texas, or California. There is no statutory framework in Arkansas governing which out-of-state agencies can access NWA plate data or for what investigative purpose.

Absence of Municipal Oversight

No NWA municipality has established a civilian oversight board, mandatory audit schedule, or public reporting requirement for ALPR deployments. Procurement decisions are typically made by police departments through operational budgets, bypassing city council approval and public comment entirely.

Data Retention Without Consent

The standard Flock Safety data retention window is 30 days, but agencies can negotiate longer retention periods. During this window, the complete travel history of every vehicle that passes a camera is searchable, regardless of whether the vehicle or its owner is under investigation.

MISSION // WHO WE ARE

About De-Flock NWA

Project Overview

De-Flock NWA is an independent, non-partisan civil watchdog project dedicated to documenting, mapping, and challenging warrantless mass vehicular surveillance across Benton and Washington Counties in Northwest Arkansas.

Automated license plate readers (ALPRs) create an inescapable dragnet of public movements, tracking everyday residents without warrants or probable cause. We believe that public safety should never come at the expense of Fourth Amendment privacy rights and community transparency.
